Heat reuse · Proof of work

Buy the heat you already need. Get the hashrate free.

A Bitcoin heater is not a gadget with a heater attached. It is a heater whose resistive element has been replaced by a computer, and the heat arrives either way. We specify, size, and commission these systems for institutions and businesses across the Northeast.

1 watt in → 3.412 BTU/hr out + hashrate

Electric resistance heating converts essentially all input energy to heat. So does an ASIC. Thermodynamically the two are the same appliance — but only one of them pays you for the privilege. Everything on this page follows from that.

Schools, universities, libraries, recreation centres, and municipal buildings share three traits that make them the strongest candidates in the entire market: a large existing electrical service, a year-round hot water demand, and a mandate to teach or demonstrate what they are doing. The heat pays for the equipment; the visibility pays for the programme.

Category 02

Small businesses

1.5 kW – 50 kW thermal

If your business already burns money on hot water or process heat every day of the year, that spend is the whole opportunity. Laundromats, breweries, salons, gyms, commercial kitchens, greenhouses, and unheated workshops all have the steady thermal load that makes the arithmetic work. Sporadic loads do not.

Category 03

Homes and small offices

300 W – 1.5 kW thermal

The retail end of the market: plug-in units that run on an ordinary 120 V circuit and need no plumbing, no permit, and no facilities staff. Also the right place to start if you want a working unit in the room before you propose anything larger to a building committee.

Category 04

Industrial and district scale

500 kW – 10 MW thermal

At megawatt scale the miner stops being an appliance and becomes a boiler. These are the deployments that have already been built and metered in Finland, British Columbia, and Manitoba, which makes them the most useful precedents to cite when a board asks whether anyone has actually done this.

Before you propose this to anyone

Where the argument is weak

Any institutional buyer worth selling to will raise these four objections, and a vendor who has not raised them first has told you something about themselves. Here they are.

A heat pump beats this on energy

Bitcoin heating has a coefficient of performance of 1.0, exactly like baseboard electric. A decent air-source heat pump delivers 2.5 to 4 units of heat per unit of electricity. Where a heat pump is viable, it wins on energy cost, and the mining revenue has to make up a gap it often cannot. The honest comparison is against resistance heat and against gas, not against the best available technology.

The revenue is volatile and trends down

Per-terahash revenue falls as network hashrate grows, and halves outright every four years at the subsidy halving. Model the heat as the product and the sats as a rebate of uncertain size. Any proposal whose payback depends on the price of Bitcoin going up is a speculation wearing an engineering costume.

Seasonal loads waste the hardware

The ASIC is the expensive part, and it only earns while it runs. A space-heating load in Broome County runs maybe five months a year; a domestic hot water load runs twelve. This is the single largest driver of payback period, and it is why water beats air almost every time.

Institutions have procurement, not preferences

Expect UL listing requirements, electrical inspection, noise limits in occupied space, IT and network security review, and in a public entity a competitive bid process. Budget more calendar time for approvals than for installation. On a SUNY campus, plan on the state fire marshal and campus IT both having standing.
